# Live motion preferences and graphics behind multiplayer — 2026-10-03

GameFrame checked decorative graphics eligibility only when gameplay initialized. An SDK-applied or player-selected reduced-motion change during play did not update the scene. A player starting with reduced motion could not enable the scene later. Opening the opaque multiplayer surface also left an existing solar scene drawing, and canceled deferred callbacks had no lifetime guard.

The frame now observes the effective motion preference through a read-only subscription and React's external-store hook. This includes platform preference application, player changes and account-state cleanup. Media-query notifications use the existing snapshot policy: an explicit applied or stored choice retains precedence over the OS default. These notifications are separate from the platform persistence subscription, so SDK reads do not create preference writes.

Enabling reduced motion unmounts the decorative scene and releases its owned resources. The containing error boundary remains mounted, retaining failed native imports and render failures. Disabling reduced motion permits an eligible deferred initial load, or restores a previously loaded healthy scene. The deferred loader retires canceled idle callbacks and fallback timers, checks the synchronous preference before publishing readiness, and does not schedule initial work while the host is paused or multiplayer is open. Once loaded, decoration pauses behind multiplayer and resumes without reconstructing its resources. Closing multiplayer preserves an independent host pause.

No visible strings, target capabilities, provider SDKs or gameplay authority rules changed.

## Remaining goal work

The broader goal remains active. An already-started native decorative download cannot be canceled by this deferred-loader guard; its resolution while multiplayer is open may still construct a paused scene. Construction deferral during pending lazy imports, graphics-driver context-restoration callbacks, safe recovery of failed interactive modules, and asynchronous gameplay/transport ownership and latency remain candidates for further investigation. This pass establishes immediate reduced-motion application and elimination of frame work behind multiplayer, rather than claiming that every hidden-surface cost has been removed.
